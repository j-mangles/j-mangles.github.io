    The blue line

    Individual short-story test · 20 marks · about 30 minutes

    Read the scene, then answer all eight questions. For written responses, a short quotation or an accurate detail counts as evidence.

    1

    By four o’clock, the hall smelled of wet wool and sawdust. Kit knelt beside the long-jump pit with a tin of blue chalk. A metre from his knee, the old school record stood on a little wooden sign. He had painted the numbers himself. They belonged to his sister.

    2

    On her last jump, Mara landed beyond the sign. Sand lifted over Kit’s shoes. He pushed a blue peg into the dent made by her heels. Behind him, the crowd began stamping the bleachers. Then he saw a second dent, closer to the board, where her hand had brushed the sand. In the morning he had read the rule aloud to the officials: measure from the nearest mark made on landing.

    3

    Kit laid the tape to the blue peg. A record by two centimetres. He measured again from the shallow handprint. Three centimetres short. He looked up at his sister. She had folded the programme so tightly that only her old number showed.

    4

    “Ready?” the announcer asked. Kit pressed the peg deeper with his thumb. The blue dust settled into the whorls of his skin. Then he pulled it out and planted it at the handprint. He held the tape until Mara came over and showed her both marks. She studied them, nodded once, and raked the pit smooth for the next jumper.

    5

    On the way out his sister tapped the wooden sign. “Another year,” she said. Kit put the chalk tin in his pocket. What he kept seeing was the rake passing over the place where his thumb had been.
