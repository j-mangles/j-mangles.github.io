Evidence from Cells

Individual test · Grade 8 Science · 20 marks

Use the field notes and model results to support each answer. Include units with your calculation.

Two tissue samples

A root sample has tightly packed cells with a firm outer wall. A cheek sample has cells with flexible outlines. Both contain nuclei and neither has visible chloroplasts.

The width of one cell

An illustrated field is 720 µm wide. About nine cell widths fit side by side across its diameter. A second objective doubles the total magnification of the same specimen.

A membrane model

Two bags each start at 12.0 g with 5% dissolved sugar inside. The membrane lets only water through. After equal time in different baths, bag X weighs 13.6 g and bag Y weighs 10.8 g. One bath was pure water and the other was 12% sugar.

Oxygen in a working tissue

A muscle begins contracting. Its cells use oxygen faster. Oxygen concentration in nearby blood is now higher than in the muscle cells. Oxygen can cross the exchange surfaces.

From cell to organ

Cardiac muscle cells contract together in the wall of the heart. Imagine that a patch of these cells can no longer contract well.
