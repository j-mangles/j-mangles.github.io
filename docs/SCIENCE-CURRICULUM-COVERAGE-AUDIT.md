# Grade 8 Science — Initial Curriculum Coverage Audit

> **Correction, 2026-11-13.** This audit measured the site against the older 2000 framework (clusters Cells and Systems, Optics, Fluids, Water Systems; outcome codes like 8-3-08). The course is planned on Manitoba's current Framework for Learning for Grade 8 Science, which covers density, solar energy, Earth's internal structure and homeostasis (outcome codes like SCI.8.E.1): https://www.edu.gov.mb.ca/k12/framework/english/science/science_gr8.html
>
> Under the current framework the Matter, Density and Fluids unit lines up with SCI.8.E.1 to E.6 lesson for lesson. Buoyancy calculations, hydraulic and pneumatic devices, mirrors and lenses, and the Water Systems cluster are **not** gaps to build toward. Do not add lessons for them on the strength of the tables below. The remaining small gaps in the Matter unit against the current outcomes are pressure units (Pa, kPa), water's melting and boiling points stated outright, and humidity and precipitation (SCI.8.E.6, SCI.8.C.2).

_Reviewed 2026-11-11. Internal teacher/engineering audit, not student-facing material._

## Scope and method

**Source of curricular requirements:** Manitoba Education and Early Childhood Learning, [Grade 8 Science Learning Outcomes](https://www.edu.gov.mb.ca/k12/cur/science/grade8_outcomes.html), [Science overview](https://www.edu.gov.mb.ca/k12/mychild/grade8_science.html), and [Grade 8 Science at a Glance](https://www.edu.gov.mb.ca/k12/cur/essentials/docs/gr8science.pdf).

**Source of site evidence:** the current \`science/data.js\` unit/lesson inventory and dedicated Science simulation files. I inspected the released/coming-soon unit structure and searched lesson text for relevant concepts, vocabulary, and activities. This is a **first-pass gap analysis**, not certification that any single outcome has been fully taught or assessed. Source booklets, teacher slide decks, actual hand-on activities, and student evidence are still needed for an outcome-by-outcome verification.

Manitoba's four Grade 8 Science clusters are **Cells and Systems, Optics, Fluids, and Water Systems**. The site's present sequence has Science Skills, Cells and Systems, Matter/Density/Fluids, Solar Energy and Light, and Earth Science/Plate Tectonics. Some website material can support the mandated clusters, but the unit names and content should not be mistaken for complete correspondence.

## High-priority Grade 8 gaps and next actions

### Cells and Systems — current teaching priority

| Outcome group | What the website currently demonstrates | Gap / needed evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 8-1-03, 8-1-05 | Cell theory, plant and animal organelles | Confirm appropriate comparison vocabulary and written student evidence |
| 8-1-06 | Microscope/magnification mentioned | Add explicit wet-mount preparation, safe handling, starting on low power, focusing and scientific drawings; simulated practice does **not** replace actual microscope use |
| 8-1-07 | Selectively permeable membranes, diffusion and osmosis | Link model trials to actual observation and data interpretation |
| 8-1-09, 8-1-10 | Specialized cells, cell-to-organ-system organization | Strong opportunities for labelled interactive structures and comparative explanations |
| 8-1-11 to 8-1-15 | Circulation, blood components, systems, oxygen model | Audit heart's four chambers and named vessels; add real before/during/after exercise heart/respiratory rate recording, with safe alternatives and teacher oversight |
| 8-1-16, 8-1-17 | Limited immune-defence material visible | Add primary/secondary defences, white blood cells, antibodies, vaccines and antibiotics; preserve scientific distinctions |
| 8-1-18, 8-1-19 | Some general health and system concepts | Add age-appropriate body-system disorders/prevention research and comparisons between different organisms |

### Optics — planned lesson expansion

**High priority:** 8-2-09 (law of reflection), 8-2-12 (concave/convex mirrors and lenses), 8-2-13 (image formation using double-convex lenses), and 8-2-14 (eye versus camera focusing). Existing Solar Energy and Light lessons contain useful prism/refraction/spectrum/EM material but do not obviously cover these outcomes in depth. Also review 8-2-02 (incandescent versus types of luminescence), 8-2-04/06 (additive/subtractive colour), and 8-2-05 (colour perception).

**Build order:**
1. Reflection-ray lab with a labelled normal line and equal incident/reflected angles. Students record and explain trials.
2. Mirror/lens comparison investigation (concave/convex). Include image-location vs object-distance predictions and model limitations.
3. Eye–camera simulator comparing aperture/pupil, lens and retina/sensor, with support for focus questions.

### Fluids — planned lesson expansion

**Existing emphasis:** density, viscosity, particle theory, temperature/volume/pressure, and some hydraulics.

**High priority:** 8-3-08 (how fluid density alters buoyancy), 8-3-11–14 (water versus air compressibility, hydraulic and pneumatic devices, advantages/trade-offs, design of a working prototype). The words *pneumatic* and *buoyant* are not clearly featured in the current fluids lesson inventory.

**Build order:**
1. Buoyancy and displaced-fluid model with proper disclaimers and sample calculations.
2. Paired hydraulic/pneumatic force-transmission simulation.
3. Prototype-planning digital notebook linked to a hands-on build, assessment criteria, and reflection.

### Water Systems — substantial curriculum gap

The Earth Science unit has useful lessons on rivers, watersheds, Red River flooding, erosion and glaciers, but this does not cover the full cluster.

| Priority | Relevant outcome IDs | Missing or thin topics to plan |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate | 8-4-02, 8-4-03, 8-4-06 | Heat capacity, dissolving substances, freshwater versus saltwater, complete global water cycle |
| High | 8-4-04, 8-4-05 | Ocean currents: convection, Coriolis effect, wind and the impact on regional climates |
| High | 8-4-07 to 8-4-13 | Continental drainage, waves, tides, river/coastal erosion, flood mitigation and impacts |
| High | 8-4-14 to 8-4-18 | Drinking-water sources, treatment, wastewater, water pollution and water-resource management |
| High | 8-4-19 | Design and test a system addressing a real water-related problem |

**Recommended decision:** retain relevant watershed/flood/glacier pieces; create a curriculum-aligned **Water Systems** unit sequence rather than implying that all plate tectonics content satisfies Cluster 4. Keep Earth Science tectonics resources as enrichment or in a separately labelled resource area as appropriate to the teacher's actual plans.

## Assessment quality checks

- A colourful simulation alone does not show the outcome was achieved. Pair each tool with prediction, controlled observation, at least 3 trials (when scientifically appropriate), explanation, and teacher-assessable work.
- For inquiry/design outcomes, document actual hands-on or authentic evidence where the outcome requires it (microscope use, optical measurements, prototype design).
- Differentiate **illustrative model numbers** from measured laboratory values. Use correct units for calculated quantities, and tell students where a model is simplified.
- In Computer Class, keep teacher-graded explanations distinct from auto-checked practice and verify cognitive load fits 30–45-minute independent periods.
- Preserve existing source handouts and release dates. Do not mass-create a new lesson simply because a keyword is absent.
