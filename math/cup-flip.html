Mystery Cup Flip

Probability · experimental probability · no login, nothing saved or submitted

Flip the cup and record where it lands. Nobody has told you the chances for this cup, so the only way to find out is to collect data.

1. Predict first

The cup can land three ways: open end up, upside down, or on its side. Before you flip anything, decide which one you think happens most often.

Experimental probability = the number of times an outcome happened ÷ the total number of flips.

3. Use your results as evidence

  1. After your first 10 flips, write the experimental probability of each outcome as a fraction and a percent.
  2. Keep flipping to 50. Which percentages changed the most? Use numbers from your table.
  3. Was your prediction from step 1 close? Say how far off it was, in flips.
  4. Compare your table with another group's. Whose numbers are closer to each other: groups at 10 flips, or groups at 50 flips?
  5. This cup has no equally likely outcomes, so you cannot calculate a theoretical probability from a sample space. Explain how you would describe the chance of landing on its side to someone who has not flipped it.

4. The long-run chances

This cup does have real chances built into it, but they only become clear after a lot of flips. Reach 200 flips and you can check how close you got.
