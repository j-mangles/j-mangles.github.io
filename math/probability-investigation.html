Grade 7 · Probability investigation

Can you trust a small sample?

Build a bag, predict what will happen, then test your prediction. Each draw puts the counter back before the next draw. No login or personal data needed.

1. Make a prediction

Start with 3 red and 7 blue counters. What fraction of draws do you expect to be red?

Explain your thinking before running the simulation. Theoretical probability = red counters ÷ total counters.

2. Run the experiment

Random results vary each time. A larger sample often gets closer to the predicted proportion, but that is not guaranteed on every run.

3. Make a claim using evidence

Complete these after 10, 100 and 1000 draws. Reset and repeat if you want another trial.

  1. Was your prediction close after 10 draws? Use a number from your results.
  2. What changed after 100 and 1000 draws? Did the results get closer to the theoretical probability?
  3. If you changed the bag to 1 red and 9 blue, what would you expect in 100 draws? Test it.
  4. Could a bag with 3 red and 7 blue produce 7 red draws in a row? Explain why this is possible or impossible.

Teacher notes

Suggested timing: 5 minutes prediction, 15 minutes trials, 10 minutes discussion, 10 minutes claim/evidence, 5 minutes exit reflection. Works as a pair activity or projector demonstration.

Outcome connection: Grade 7 probability: express probability as a fraction or percent, compare theoretical and experimental probabilities, and reason about experimental sample size. For the full curriculum sequence, return to the Probability unit.

Exit slip: “Why can 10 draws be misleading even when we know the probability?”
