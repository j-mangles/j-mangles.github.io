1. Who gets to vote?

The Assembly meets tomorrow. In 440 BCE, a voting citizen had to be an adult man with two Athenian parents. Wealth and literacy did not determine voting rights.

2. The mystery in the well

Archaeological evidence

In 1937, archaeologists in Athens found 190 pottery pieces in an old well. Each had Themistocles' name scratched on it. When experts compared the writing, they found only 14 different hands. The sherds seem never to have been used.

Based on Oscar Broneer's 1938 excavation report. An ostrakon was a pottery fragment used as a ballot in an ostracism, a vote to exile a citizen for ten years.

3. Two views of democracy

Source A · Pericles, 431 BCE

“Its administration favours the many instead of the few; this is why it is called a democracy. If we look to the laws, they afford equal justice to all…”

Thucydides, History of the Peloponnesian War 2.37, translated by Richard Crawley (1874). Thucydides notes that speeches are reconstructed to express their general sense.

Source B · Plato, about 375 BCE (paraphrased)

Imagine a ship where the sailors vote on who should steer. They choose whoever flatters them and promises the most, and laugh at the trained navigator who studies the stars. Plato compares this with a city governed by a crowd.
