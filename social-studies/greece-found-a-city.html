Found a City

It is about 650 BCE. Your polis has more people than its land can feed. Choose a place for 200 settlers, using evidence from four scouts' reports.

All four sites are real. Your scoring is a reasoned choice, not a single-answer test. Your writing stays on this device until you download it.

1. What does a new polis need?

Score each site for every need: 3 = excellent, 2 = workable, 1 = a problem. Use the report details to decide. The totals help you compare, but they cannot make the decision for you.

Site A · Island and plain

A small island sits just off the coast, close enough to bridge. It has a freshwater spring beside the sea and a sheltered harbour on each side. Across the water is a wide, fertile plain. People already live there and are not pleased to see ships arriving.

Site B · Rocky inlet

A deep, rocky inlet makes a calm natural harbour. The land is thin and stony, better for vines and olive trees than grain. A short sail away, a great river runs far inland, and the people there are keen to trade metals for wine.

Site C · Busy strait

A point of land juts into a narrow strait joining two seas. Every grain ship must pass within sight. The harbour is superb and the fishing is excellent. Inland tribes are hostile, and the currents in the strait are strong.

Site D · High plateau

A high plateau about 10 km from the coast has a strong spring, rich soil and more rain than anywhere else on this dry shore. It has no harbour, so goods must be carried up from the beach. Earlier settlers on an offshore island nearly starved.

3. Reveal the real places

First record your choice and reasons. Then match the scout reports to the ancient settlements. The reports are intentionally balanced: there is no one correct site to choose.

Site A

Syracuse

Still called Syracuse (Siracusa), on Sicily. The fertile plain was already inhabited; settlement involved encounters with local people.

Site B

Massalia

Modern Marseille, France. A strong harbour connected Greek settlers with trade along the Rhône and its hinterland.

Site C

Byzantium

Later Constantinople; today Istanbul, Turkey. Its position beside the Bosporus made it strategically important for traffic between seas.

Site D

Cyrene

Ruins near Shahhat, Libya. The inland plateau offered fertile land and a spring, though the coast remained important for access.

These colonies were independent cities, not territories governed by a Greek empire. Local communities already lived in many places where settlers arrived.
