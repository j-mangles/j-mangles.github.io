Egypt’s Long Goodbye

← Egypt atlas

Egypt lost independence in stages, while parts of its culture continued. Build the conquest timeline, connect causes with C.R.A.S.H., then decide what “the end of ancient Egypt” means.

01 · LINE THEM UP

Six powers take control

Place each episode in chronological order. Dates are BCE: the larger number is earlier.

02 · CONNECT CAUSES

C.R.A.S.H. was not one single cause

Choose the category that best fits each detail. Some can fit more than one; be ready to explain your choice. “Sickness” may have little evidence in this case.

03 · DEFINE AN ENDING

When did ancient Egypt end?

Each date marks a different kind of change. Choose the date that best matches your definition of “ancient Egypt,” then test your idea against another date.

1070 BCE

Egypt splits in two and never regains its old power.

343 BCE

The last Egyptian-born pharaoh loses his throne.

30 BCE

Cleopatra dies; Egypt becomes a Roman province.

394 CE

The last hieroglyphic inscription is carved at Philae.

642 CE

Arab armies take Egypt; a new language and religion follow.

04 · TRACE A LEGACY

What did Egypt leave behind?

Choose one Egyptian legacy—365-day calendar, 24-hour day, medicine, papyrus/paper, or ideas borrowed from hieroglyphs—and trace how it reaches your life today. Then read the final lines of Shelley’s “Ozymandias”: “Nothing beside remains.” Do you agree?
