The Governor's Problem

About 112 CE, Pliny was governor of a Roman province in what is now Turkey. People were being reported to him as Christians. He wrote to the emperor Trajan to explain his decisions and ask what to do.

Read the sources as historical evidence. The activity describes beliefs without asking you to accept or reject them. Your writing stays here until downloaded.

1. What Pliny did

Pliny, letter to the emperor Trajan, about 112 CE
“...the method I have observed towards those who have been brought before me as Christians is this: I asked them whether they were Christians; if they admitted it, I repeated the question twice, and threatened them with punishment; if they persisted, I ordered them to be at once punished: for I was persuaded, whatever the nature of their opinions might be, a contumacious and inflexible obstinacy certainly deserved correction.”
“An anonymous information was laid before me containing a charge against several persons, who upon examination denied they were Christians, or had ever been so. They repeated after me an invocation to the gods, and offered religious rites with wine and incense before your statue ... and even reviled the name of Christ: whereas there is no forcing, it is said, those who are really Christians into any of these compliances: I thought it proper, therefore, to discharge them.”

Pliny, Letters 10.96, translated by William Melmoth and revised by F. C. T. Bosanquet (1909, public domain). Dots mark omitted words. Contumacious means stubbornly disobedient; discharge means let go.

2. You are the emperor

Make your own rulings before reading Trajan's reply. For each question, choose a policy and explain why. The reply stays hidden until all three decisions and reasons are entered.

Complete all three rulings and give a reason for each before revealing the response.

3. Trajan replies

Trajan's instructions, about 112 CE
  • Do not go out of your way to search for Christians.
  • If someone is formally accused and proven to be a Christian, punish them; if they deny it and perform the public rites, pardon them.
  • Do not accept anonymous accusations.

Trajan's answer tries to prevent disturbance while still allowing punishment after a formal accusation. The policy is difficult to square: if it was a serious crime, why not search for it? The letters show decisions in one period and province, not one fixed rule applied everywhere.

5. Why did it spread?

Choose the two factors you think mattered most, then explain both. The choices can work together; make a case for your selection.

Persecution was local and intermittent in many earlier periods; it became more systematic during the Great Persecution that began in 303 CE. Historians distinguish between explaining religious change and making a judgement about religious beliefs.
