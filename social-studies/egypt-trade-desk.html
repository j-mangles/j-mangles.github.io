Value was measured in deben, a set weight of copper. A price could be counted in deben even when the actual exchange used grain, oil, or cloth.

Three clues, three kinds of evidence

Clue A · Written and carved records

Egyptian texts and temple carvings say ships sailed south on the Red Sea from an Egyptian harbour.

Clue B · Goods from Punt

Expeditions brought back myrrh, incense, ebony, ivory, gold, and live baboons.

Clue C · Baboon remains

Chemical traces in mummified baboons point to the region of modern Eritrea, Ethiopia, and northwest Somalia.

Uluburun: a shipwreck from about 1300 BCE

Around ten tonnes of copper, tin, ebony, ivory, pottery, resin, and a scarab with Nefertiti’s name came from different places. Compare shipwreck cargo with the Amarna letters between kings.

What Egypt borrowed

During the Second Intermediate Period, the Hyksos brought horses, chariots, and stronger bows. Egyptians kept these technologies after driving them out.
