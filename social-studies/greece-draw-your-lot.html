1. Compare the two poleis

Use the chart from the lesson slides or open this reference when you need it.

Open the Athens and Sparta comparison chart
TopicAthensSparta
GovernmentDemocracy: citizens voted on laws in the AssemblyOligarchy: two kings, five ephors and a council of elders
EconomySea trade, crafts and silver minesFarming by helots; citizen men were not supposed to trade
Military strengthNavyArmy
What it praisedFreedom, debate, art and new ideasDiscipline, strength, obedience and tradition
BoysPrivate schooling if a family could pay; military training at 18State training (agoge) from 7; barracks until 30
Girls and womenNo formal schooling; most women had limited public roles and could not voteGirls trained outdoors; women could own land and had more public roles, but could not vote
Forced labourEnslaved people were bought and sold; work included homes, workshops and minesHelots were unfree and farmed the land; the state controlled them
OutsidersForeign residents could live and trade but could not votePerioikoi in nearby towns could trade and serve in the army but had no political power in Sparta

Both poleis excluded women from political power and depended on forced labour, although the systems were different.
