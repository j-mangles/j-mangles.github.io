Grade 8 Social Studies · Mesopotamia · Evidence Tour

Flying Over Mesopotamia

Follow the Tigris and Euphrates from their mountain sources to the Persian Gulf. At six stops, collect evidence about rivers, cities, trade, power and the traces people leave behind.

Google Earth shows the present-day landscape. Keep present observations separate from claims about ancient life; use the lesson notes and other evidence to connect them. Your written responses stay in this browser tab until you download them.

Before you start
  1. Open Google Earth Web in a new tab.
  2. Search the place named at each stop. Zoom in, look around, and use the ruler tool where asked.
  3. Record what you can actually observe. If a feature is unclear, write that down instead of guessing.

If Google Earth is unavailable, use the Mesopotamia Atlas for place locations and complete the reasoning prompts from class notes. The Atlas is a schematic historical map, not satellite imagery.

Stop 1 · The land between the rivers

Trace the Tigris and Euphrates

Open Iraq in Google Earth ↗

Zoom out until both rivers are visible from their headwaters to the Gulf. Then follow them upstream and downstream.

Evidence check: These rivers cross modern national borders. Name the country and landscape you can see; do not infer ancient borders from today's map.

Sketch the region from your observation

Stop 2 · Uruk

A city beside a river that moved

Open Uruk in Google Earth ↗

Uruk grew beside the Euphrates and became one of the largest cities of its time. The river's channel has shifted over thousands of years.

Stop 3 · Ur

A temple built to stand out

Open the Ziggurat of Ur ↗

Tilt and orbit the ziggurat. It was built around 2100 BCE, after Enheduanna's lifetime, and parts of its front wall and staircase were rebuilt in the 1980s.

Stop 4 · Baghdad

Where Sargon's capital may have been

Open Baghdad in Google Earth ↗

Akkad, Sargon's capital, has not been located. Archaeologists place the search area near where the Tigris and Euphrates draw closer, north of the southern Sumerian cities.

Stop 5 · Babylon

A city shaped by its river

Open Babylon in Google Earth ↗

Hammurabi ruled from Babylon roughly five centuries after Sargon. The site includes ancient remains alongside modern reconstruction.

Stop 6 · Al-Qurnah

Follow the rivers to the Gulf

Open Al-Qurnah in Google Earth ↗

Follow the Tigris and Euphrates downstream and trace the water toward the Persian Gulf.

Put the evidence together

How did the rivers shape life in Mesopotamia?

Make a clear claim, then use evidence from at least three stops. Explain how the evidence supports your claim; distinguish what you observed today from what you infer about the past.

Optional extension · Ishtar Gate

Search “Ishtar Gate Berlin.” Part of Babylon's monumental gate is now in a museum in Germany. Who built, moved and now cares for it? Where do you think it belongs, and what evidence or perspective would help you answer fairly?
