Grade 8 Social Studies · Ancient Greece · Lesson 8

Follow the Money

Trace how a shared alliance fund became part of Athenian power. Then weigh the arguments about who benefited from the buildings raised on the Acropolis.

The ancient people used in the writing prompt are imagined. The historical events and debate are paraphrased from the class materials. Your work stays in this browser tab until you download it.

1. Track the fund

Put the events in order. Use each number once. Ask when the alliance was protecting its members, and when Athens was deciding what members could do.

EventOrder
Greek cities form an alliance against Persia and keep their shared treasury on Delos.
Many members pay silver instead of sending ships.
Naxos tries to leave the alliance. Athens forces it back.
The treasury moves from Delos to Athens.
Pericles proposes using the league fund to rebuild temples on the Acropolis.
The Parthenon is completed.

2. Examine the debate

The critics' case

They argued that allied cities had contributed money to resist Persia, but Athens was using the shared fund to decorate its own city. They feared the allies would see this as theft.

Pericles' defence

He argued that the allies paid money rather than supplying ships or soldiers, that Athens protected them, and that the building work paid many workers.

This is a classroom paraphrase of a debate reported by Plutarch, who wrote about five centuries after the events. It is evidence of how the argument was remembered, not a recording of the original speeches.

3. Write from a perspective

Choose an imagined person. Use at least two facts from the activity and make the person's point of view clear.

If you choose a stonemason, specify whether the person is a citizen, a foreign resident or enslaved; do not assume every worker had the same status.

4. Make a case about the Parthenon sculptures

These are arguments to examine, not a quiz with one required verdict. Consider removal, cultural connection, preservation and public access.

Return to Athens

  • The sculptures were removed while Greece was under Ottoman rule.
  • They formed part of a building and could be understood with the Parthenon.
  • A museum near the Acropolis can display them in that setting.

Remain in London

  • Elgin received a permit from Ottoman authorities; historians disagree about what it authorized.
  • Supporters argue museum care protects the sculptures from weathering; removal and transport also caused damage.
  • Many visitors can see them alongside objects from different cultures.
