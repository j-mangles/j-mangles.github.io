Reading a Mesopotamian City

Individual test · Grade 8 Social Studies · 20 marks

Use the evidence files and your unit knowledge. Give specific details and explain how they support your ideas.

Water beyond the riverbank

File A · Farming and work. In southern Mesopotamia, farmers directed river water into canals and cleared silt from the channels. A harvest could produce more grain than the farming household used.

A record from the storehouse

File B · Record keeping. Early tablets from Uruk recorded quantities of goods. A much later tablet from Ur preserves Nanni’s complaint to the copper merchant Ea-nasir.

A ruler and a law

File C · Hammurabi’s stele. The carving shows King Hammurabi facing Shamash, the god of justice. The written penalties for a bodily injury could differ according to the social rank of the injured person.

Three dates

File D · Approximate dates. Hammurabi’s laws, 1750 BCE. Early writing at Uruk, 3300 BCE. Sargon’s empire, 2334 BCE.

Whose city do we see?

File E · The Standard of Ur. In one scene, seated figures feast while others carry goods and animals. Nanni’s complaint in File B records a buyer’s view of an exchange.
