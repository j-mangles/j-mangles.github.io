Egyptians imagined death as a journey that required preparation. Work through the mummification sequence, read a source with a historian’s caution, and make a burial plan with a limited budget.

01 · THE 70-DAY PROCESS

Put the embalmers’ steps in order

Use each number once. Egyptians believed the heart was needed at the judgment, while the brain was not used for thinking.

Remove the brain
Remove four organs through a cut in the left side
Leave the heart in place
Pack the body in natron salt to dry
Dry the organs and place them in canopic jars
Wrap the body in linen with amulets

Why preserve the body? In the lesson, the ka (life force) stayed with the body and the ba (personality) returned at night. The body gave both a place to return to. A balanced heart meant entering the Field of Reeds, a perfect Egypt; the heart was kept for weighing against Ma’at’s feather.

02 · READ A SOURCE CAREFULLY

What can a declaration tell us?

“I have not stolen grain.” — Book of the Dead, Spell 125, from the Papyrus of Ani, made for a scribe at Thebes around 1250 BCE. This is a declaration spoken before the judges in the afterlife.

04 · COMPARE EXPECTATIONS

Egypt and Mesopotamia

In a short response, compare one Egyptian belief about the afterlife with the Mesopotamian underworld. Include a detail from each society and explain what the difference suggests about how they viewed life after death.
