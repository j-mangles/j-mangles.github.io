Source A · Strabo

A Greek geographer writing around 20 CE, while living in the Roman Empire. He describes the queen of Kush who fought Rome and comments on her appearance.

Source B · Bronze head of Augustus

A larger-than-life bronze head of Rome’s emperor was found buried beneath the top step of a temple at Meroë, the capital of Kush. People entering would walk over it.

Your verdict

Were Egypt and Nubia rivals, trading partners, or both?

One evidence path to considerEgypt and Nubia were both rivals and trading partners. The record shows power moving in both directions: Kerma was a powerful neighbour; Egypt later ruled Nubia; Kushite rulers then governed Egypt; and Meroë remained an independent centre. Trade and cultural exchange continued across many of these changes. Egyptian and Roman sources survive more often than accounts from Nubians themselves, so our view is incomplete.

Based on the student task “Rivals or Partners?” in the Egypt and Nubia lesson. A source can provide useful evidence while still reflecting the maker’s point of view.
