3. Phalanx against legion

Complete the comparison from the lesson. Then reveal a study check.

QuestionGreek phalanxRoman legion
How was it arranged?
Main weapons?
Where did it fight best?
Biggest weakness?

Phalanx: one close block with long spears; strongest on flat, open ground; vulnerable if rough ground or a flank disrupted it.

Legion: smaller units could move separately; pilum then short sword; more flexible on varied terrain, though a phalanx could be powerful head-on on flat ground.

4. Question the source

Polybius, Histories 6.37, about 150 BCE
“The tribune takes a cudgel and just touches the condemned man with it, after which all in the camp beat or stone him, in most cases dispatching him in the camp itself. ... Thus, owing to the extreme severity and inevitableness of the penalty, the night watches of the Roman army are most scrupulously kept.”

Translated by W. R. Paton (1923, public domain). This describes punishment for a soldier who failed on night watch. Cudgel means a heavy stick; dispatching means killing.
