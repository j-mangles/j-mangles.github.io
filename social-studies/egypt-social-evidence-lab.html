Unit 2 · Ancient Egypt · Lesson 7

Who Benefited from Egypt?

← Egypt atlas

Use small, specific records to test big claims about Egyptian society. Evidence can support one part of a claim and challenge another; a single person's story may not represent everyone.

01 · TEST THE CLAIMS

Build a balanced evidence file

For each claim, choose evidence that supports it and evidence that challenges it. The same card can be used more than once. Then make a verdict and explain it.

Open the nine student evidence cards
A · Naunakhte’s will

Around 1145 BCE, she went to court and removed four of her eight children from her will. The court accepted her decision.

B · Women’s property

Egyptian women could divorce and keep the property they brought into a marriage.

C · Government jobs

Almost no women held government jobs, and nearly all scribes were men.

D · Farmers and taxes

Farmers paid part of their harvest in taxes. Some officials’ tomb scenes show people who could not pay being beaten.

E · Labour tax

During the flood season, farmers could be called up to work on royal building projects.

F · The workers’ strike

Deir el-Medina workers stopped work when grain payments were late and eventually got paid.

G · Scribe school

Some boys could rise into government jobs, but most scribes were the sons of scribes.

H · Adoption Papyrus

Around 1100 BCE, Rennefer freed three children born to an enslaved woman in her household and made them her heirs.

I · War captives

In the New Kingdom, war captives were brought to Egypt and forced to work for the king and temples.

Claim 1 · “Egyptian women had real legal rights.”

Claim 2 · “Ordinary Egyptians had no power at all.”

Claim 3 · “Where you were born decided almost everything about your life.”
